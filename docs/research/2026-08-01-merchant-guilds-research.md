# Merchant Guilds Research

## Progression & Meta-Game

**Unlock structure and ordering.** Progression in Merchant: Guilds is gated almost entirely through **building levels**, not a linear "story" unlock track. Eight buildings (Crafter's Circle, Tavern, Workers' Barracks, Heroes' Hall, Shop, World Gate, Warehouse, Guild Hall) each unlock different content as they level: the Tavern reveals new hireable heroes (3 heroes at level 1, expanding up through level 16 for the last one), the Crafter's Circle reveals new crafting professions, and the World Gate/Workers' Barracks gate access to entire new regions (Tuvale is available immediately; Yarsol needs Town Gate 9+ or Barracks 7+; Aldur needs Town Gate 20+ or Barracks 13+) ([Buildings](https://merchantguilds.wiki.gg/wiki/Buildings), [Heroes](https://merchantguilds.wiki.gg/wiki/Heroes), [Regions](https://merchantguilds.wiki.gg/wiki/Regions)). Because two different buildings can each unlock the same region, players have some choice in *which* path they invest in, giving a light sense of build variety even though the endpoint is fixed.

**Guild-gated progression (the meta-game's biggest risk).** Building upgrades are funded communally: players contribute gold (non-refundable, permanent) or materials (once/day per building), and a guild-wide multiplier scales with how many guildmates contribute. Critically, each player only receives the *personal* benefit tier matching their own contribution, even if the guild's building is higher-level — a soft anti-freeloading design ([Guild Hall](https://merchantguilds.wiki.gg/wiki/Guild)). On top of building levels, guilds also compete in weekly **Guild Leagues**, climbing brackets for guild-wide bonuses. This is the most-repeated critique in community feedback: reviewers and the fan wiki both flag that tying core progression to guild activity means "one active player leaving the game can keep an entire guild from progressing," and that in practice high-contribution players get funneled into a small number of top guilds, leaving everyone else stuck. One long-time player review was blunt: with only 2 of 9 guild members active, further progress felt "pointless."

**Prestige / reset loops.** No evidence of any prestige, rebirth, ascension, or reset mechanic was found anywhere on the wiki (Guild Hall, Heroes, Achievements, Buildings, or the wiki's main landing page) or in reviews. Hero progression is persistent and additive — heroes keep XP, skills, and gear across re-hires — and the closest thing to a "prestige" layer is a large **Achievement** system (Milestones, Enemy kills, Hero usage, Crafter volume, Customer sales, Misc) that grants gold/item rewards and cosmetic titles (e.g., "Goblin Slayer," "Divine Warrior") at escalating tiers, up to very high counts (10,000 crafts, 1,000,000 gold, 10,000 consecutive quest wins) ([Achievements](https://merchantguilds.wiki.gg/wiki/Achievements)). This functions as a long-tail engagement hook rather than a true reset loop — it stretches endgame goals without ever letting players reset for a fresh, faster run.

**Session length and pacing.** The game is explicitly built for flexible session lengths — one App Store reviewer noted "you can sit there and play for many hours or check back in sporadically," and Google Play feedback similarly reported people playing anywhere from ~10 minutes to a couple hours daily. Pacing is controlled less by classic energy/stamina timers and more by **quest-area exhaustion with a reset timer** (locations run dry and refresh on a cooldown) and by the daily-once material-donation cap per building. The idle/offline-progress framing ("grow your guild even when you're not playing") supports low-commitment check-ins, but the moment-to-moment interaction loop drew consistent complaints for lacking automation: reviewers described excessive manual taps for repetitive actions ("Tap the map. Tap a map location. Tell your hero to attack. Confirm the attack." / "Do I really need to craft just one iron sword, wait, accept it, then ask to make it again?") and grind that boils down to sending a hero to "kill a beetle 30 times so you can make them better gear."

**Long-term engagement — what worked vs. what caused drop-off.** What players praised: depth/personalization in the systems, and the classic incremental-game pull of "numbers getting bigger and better resources being unlocked." What caused drop-off: (1) the guild-dependency problem above, which turns a solo player's engagement into a hostage of their guildmates' activity; (2) tap-heavy UI friction with no bulk/auto actions for repetitive tasks; and (3) per an App Store review, the game itself was reported "abandoned" by its lead developer with an inactive Discord, which — while a dev/support issue rather than a mechanic — compounded the guild-dependency problem since a shrinking playerbase makes it harder to find/keep an active guild.

### Implications for MerchantNext

- **Avoid hard-gating core progression behind other players' activity.** Merchant Guilds' single most-repeated complaint is that guild-shared building levels make an individual's progress hostage to squadmates who log off. If MerchantNext has any co-op/guild layer, make it an accelerant (bonus multipliers, cosmetic competition) layered on top of a fully-completable solo progression track — never the only path to core unlocks.
- **Use multiple parallel gates, not a single linear ladder, for unlocks.** Letting players choose *which* building/path to invest in to reach the same regional unlock (Town Gate vs. Workers' Barracks) gave a sense of agency without actually branching content. Consider offering 2+ routes to the same major unlock so different playstyles (combat-heavy vs. economy-heavy) each feel like a valid "way in."
- **Build in a real prestige/reset loop — Merchant Guilds has none, and its endgame answer (achievement grinding to 10,000+ counts) is a stretch, not a reset.** A soft-reset/rebirth system that returns meaningfully faster or different second-time-through play would fill a real gap this genre's players seem to want but didn't get here.
- **Design the tap loop for bulk/auto actions from day one.** The single most specific mechanical complaint was needing to confirm each craft and each combat step individually. Batch-crafting, auto-confirm toggles, or "send hero to farm N times" queuing directly addresses the top usability complaint without changing the underlying economy.
- **Keep pacing gates (area cooldowns, daily caps) but make them feel like a reason to log in, not a wall.** The area-exhaustion/reset-timer approach supported healthy short-session check-ins; keep that shape, but pair it with enough offline/idle yield that a missed day doesn't compound into guild-drag the way Merchant Guilds' contribution model does.
- **If a Discord/live-service layer is planned, its health is part of the progression design.** A user-reported "abandoned by lead dev, dead Discord" complaint directly undercut the guild-progression system in the wild — for MerchantNext, treat sustained dev/community presence as a dependency of any multiplayer-gated progression feature, or don't gate progression on it at all.

## Reception & Pain Points

**What players praised.** Across both the original *Merchant* (2015, Steam, 78% positive of 604 reviews) and its mobile sequel *Merchant: Guilds*, the recurring praise is the same: a game that looks like simple pixel-art clicking but hides real strategic depth — battle logs, gear optimization, party composition, and a satisfying "numbers go up" crafting/hero progression loop that reviewers called addictive ("An hour in, I thought, wow this is cool… one hundred and fifty hours in, and I no longer think, or sleep, or breathe") ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/346750/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&filterLanguage=english)). Players also credited flexible pacing — playable in short idle check-ins or long active sessions ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/merchant-guilds/id1526555919)) — and the amount of personalization in crafting/hero systems.

**What players complained about.**
- **Manual-action fatigue / lack of automation.** The single most specific, repeated complaint: too much tapping for too little payoff. One App Store review titled *"Doesn't need to be an MMO. Does need less tapping, more auto-repeating"* walks through the exact friction loop — "Tap the map. Tap a map location. Tell your hero to attack. Confirm the attack. Tap to see the results. Tap to accept the results" — and a Steam review of the original independently makes the same complaint: *"No automation… this is exhausting and annoying"* despite heavy grinding requirements ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/merchant-guilds/id1526555919); [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/346750/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&filterLanguage=english)).
- **Mobile-to-PC port friction.** Steam reviewers of *Merchant* repeatedly flagged the game as a direct mobile port that never adapted its UI for desktop — small/portrait-mode windows, forced scrolling, inability to see all heroes/crafters at once ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/346750/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&filterLanguage=english)).
- **Complexity creep.** Multiple reviewers said the game looks simple early on but becomes "very confusing with a lot going on" and "overwhelming" as systems stack up in the midgame.
- **Monetization skepticism, specifically around the live-service angle.** A reviewer directly challenged the studio's "ethical free-to-play"/no-P2W marketing, and questioned paying $35 for an "all upgrades" bundle in a game that depends on servers staying up: *"Why would I spend $35 for 'all upgrades' if that's going to shut down some day? What does this actually gain from being an MMO?"* — concluding *"I liked the original Merchant when it released but wow have the wrong lessons been taken from how to improve on that…"* ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/merchant-guilds/id1526555919)).
- **Guild/multiplayer dependency as a design regression.** The same reviewer thread frames the pivot from the original's solo tycoon loop to a guild-centric sequel as an unwanted change in kind, not just degree — echoed by other reviews noting the sequel "took away the simplistic controls and layouts" that made the original click.
- **Dev responsiveness perception.** At least one Steam review of the original reported a game-breaking bug left unresolved for months, and separately expressed concern the project looked abandoned ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/346750/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&filterLanguage=english)). This should be read as a *perception* data point, not a confirmed fact — the original *Merchant* has in fact kept receiving patches for years (most recently v1.21 patch notes dated Jan 2026), so the "abandoned" read seems to stem from slow/quiet communication rather than truly halted development ([Steam news](https://steamcommunity.com/app/346750/allnews/)).

**What the developers identified and changed.** Retora's own dev update is unusually candid about what the original got wrong. In their update explaining why *Merchant Guilds* slipped past its 2021 target, they say the sequel isn't just "multiplayer bolted onto the original" but a rebuild driven by "five years of lessons" — specifically citing the need for **Worker NPCs to handle resource gathering** so players aren't forced into repetitive combat loops for materials, and **Crafter Queues for batch production** instead of one-item-at-a-time crafting ([Mailchimp update](https://mailchi.mp/retoragames/merchant-guilds-update)). That directly maps onto the "too much tapping / no automation" complaints above. Later patch notes for the mobile build show the same instinct applied to shop/UI flow: batch "multi sell from shop" for any table type, claiming *all* available items in a crafter queue in one tap instead of one at a time, and switching world/region map zoom from touch-gesture to a button (implying the original touch-zoom was itself a UX complaint) ([Google Play, patch notes](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.retora.merchantguilds)). Retora also ran at least one explicit community-informed rebalance, "Balance Pass in Merchant 1.1" (2018), publicly crediting a specific player for shaping the changes ([itch.io devlog](https://retora-games.itch.io/merchant/devlog)), and its Steam patch history over 2018–2022 shows several QoL features (repeat-quest button, mouse-scroll support, save slots) added directly in response to player requests ([Steam news](https://steamcommunity.com/app/346750/allnews/)). The tradeoff Retora made explicitly: they chose to delay years rather than "release a half finished mess" — a quality-over-speed stance that, per the reviews above, itself read to some players as stalled/abandoned development.

### Implications for MerchantNext

- **Ship batch actions from day one, not as a later patch.** "Claim all," "sell all," and queued/batch crafting were things Retora had to retrofit in over years after sustained tap-fatigue complaints. Build multi-select/batch-resolve into the core interaction loop (crafting queue, sell flow, quest resolution) before launch rather than shipping a one-tap-per-item loop and fixing it reactively.
- **Automate rote resource-gathering, reserve manual interaction for decisions that matter.** Retora's own retrospective names repetitive manual combat-for-materials as a design mistake they fixed with idle/worker NPCs. Default MerchantNext's gathering/production loops to passive or low-touch, and spend the player's active clicks on choices (pricing, hero builds, crafting priorities) rather than repetition.
- **If any multiplayer/guild layer exists, make it additive, not a replacement for the solo loop.** The sharpest, most quotable complaint in this research was aimed specifically at forcing an MMO/guild structure onto what was a beloved solo tycoon experience. Keep the core shopkeeper loop fully satisfying solo; treat social/guild features as optional multipliers, not gates.
- **Design UI/information density per target platform, don't port one layout everywhere.** If MerchantNext targets more than one screen size/input method, avoid a single portrait-phone-style layout with forced scrolling — this was a named complaint on the desktop port of the original.
- **Be conservative and transparent with any "no pay-to-win" or live-service monetization messaging.** Reviewers scrutinize this framing closely and will call it out as marketing spin if a large one-time purchase is tied to something that could later disappear (e.g., always-online/guild dependency). If MerchantNext has any monetization or live component, make the value and permanence of any purchase legible up front.
- **Communicate development pace even when it's slow.** The original's continued (if quiet) multi-year patch cadence was read by some players as abandonment simply due to low visibility. Regular, even small, public updates likely matter as much as the underlying dev velocity for reception.
- **Keep the early game legible, let depth emerge later — but watch the complexity ramp.** The praise pattern ("looks simple, surprisingly deep") is a real asset; the complaint pattern ("overwhelming as it progresses") is the same asset turning into a liability. Budget explicit onboarding/complexity-pacing design so systemic depth doesn't outrun the player's mental model.
